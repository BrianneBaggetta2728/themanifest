The approach, since October 2026

What changed

We were going to build the whole platform before any Client saw it. Months of building, nothing new to touch. We are not doing that anymore.

Portal 1 keeps doing what it does today: orders, proofs, invoices, production, and delivery tracking. Our new platform sits in front of it and talks to it behind the scenes. Nobody logs into Portal 1 anymore, and no Client ever sees it.

Why

The Frasier Storefront. We built a real Client-facing site in a few weeks with Portal 1 running behind it, and it worked.

Building it showed us where the real pain is: nobody can see which designs exist or how they are built, and our Client data lives in spreadsheets. The floor is not the problem. Production runs well today and can wait.

What it buys us

  • Clients get off Portal 1 in weeks, not months
  • Every Client gets a new home: a Storefront or the App
  • Client Services and the design team get one place to work, now
  • What we build now is the real thing; nothing gets rebuilt later
  • Our energy goes to Clients and revenue, not plumbing

The order of operations

  1. Build the pieces Portal 1 is bad at: products, designs, Client records, mailing lists
  2. Command Center V1 for our team, on top of those
  3. Frasier moves onto the new platform first, then Audibel
  4. Storefronts become a template; the App launches for everyone else
  5. Billing, rebates, and our own design editor come next
  6. Production moves last; then Portal 1 shuts down

No launch date. The order is the plan.

The Engine RoomBack of House

Ten engines plus ORB, one model. Engines never show their names to a Client or an employee; they publish contracts, and the Interfaces above render them. Each tile carries its V1 build marker and links to the ERD, where its tables are drawn.

Why the dictionary and the ERD. Every table the platform will have is named once and drawn once before anyone writes code.Read more

The Shared Data Dictionary names every entity, field, and picklist, with the decision behind each. The ERD is the dictionary turned into a picture: one block per engine, every relationship citing the row that states it, every table marked Build now, Stored from Portal 1, or Model only. Build now means the table is built and filled in V1. Stored from Portal 1 means the table exists but Portal 1 writes it through the service layer and we only read it. Model only means the full table is created empty, so nothing is renamed or rebuilt later. One rule for the build: every ratified row in the dictionary must map to a named element in the built schema. If a row has no home in the code, the build is not done.

Data Dictionary Ledger

ORB Stored from Portal 1, then Model only

Owns every order from submission to the mailbox: intake, merge into Jobs, Job Sheets, Drops, delivery tracking, the permanent record. The spine every other engine plugs into. In V1 Portal 1 does this work; the platform stores and shows the Orders, proofs, invoices, and delivery it creates, and Campaigns live here from day one. ORB takes production last.

In the ERD

The Floor Model only

A module inside ORB: production execution, department reports, machines, inventory, floor screens. If the Floor goes down, orders still come in. Production stays on the systems that work today until ORB is ready.
